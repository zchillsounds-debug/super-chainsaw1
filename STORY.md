# Madinat al-Salam: story bible (Round 11; Round 23: the Byzantine mission)

The inspiration is Shia storytelling, carried by **themes only**. There are no names of holy figures, no rituals, no religious buildings, symbols or text.

## Themes
- **Standing with the wronged against the powerful.** The strong want silence; the weak have only memory.
- **Loyalty that costs everything.** Some keep faith with a dead man's cause; others sell it for bread.
- **Remembrance as resistance.** To speak the names of the fallen is to refuse the tyrant's version of events.
- **Thirst.** Denying water is the cruellest act in the story.
- **Awaited justice.** The scales will be set right one day. Until then we keep the account.

## The secret of the caravan
Ishaq's instruments were not the only cargo. A small cedar chest held the **Pages**, the collected sayings and letters of a gentle scholar called only **the Teacher**. He died in a prison by the Tigris fourteen years ago, in 799, and his students say he was poisoned. Ishaq was the Teacher's student.

## The frame (Round 23)
While the caliph's two sons fight over Baghdad, an envoy from Constantinople, **Arsaber**, slips into Iraq under the smoke of their war with a picked company of soldiers and hired turncoats. His orders are to carry the best learning of Baghdad home to Constantinople, and the Teacher's Pages are the prize. (Historically no Byzantine army reached Baghdad in 813, which is why this is a covert mission and not an invasion.) The civil war stays the backdrop, not the enemy. Every adversary is Byzantine (al-Rum): period Greek names, no saints' or biblical names, single names without epithets.

## Characters
- **Salim:** a caravan guard. He is practical, quiet, and not a believer in causes until the cause is his brother's last request.
- **Jabir:** Salim's older brother and captain of the caravan guard. He dies in the ambush with an arrow in the chest, loosed by Tatzates. His last words are "The chest. Do not let them burn it."
- **Ishaq:** an astronomer. He grieves without spectacle, and his line is "We keep the account."
- **Arsaber:** the envoy. He came for learning the way other men come for gold. He holds the river quays himself in Act VI.
- **Tatzates:** an Armenian bowman in Byzantine pay, who shot Jabir. "Bardanes paid. I shot. That is all it was." He escapes three times and makes his last stand in his Hamrin hold.
- **Photeinos** (Act I): one of the company. He took the envoy's gold, then read a page by firelight and never slept well again. His dying words name Bardanes and Olbianos; the banner names the envoy, Arsaber.
- **Olbianos** (Act II): a Greek copyist who once sat at the Teacher's lessons, and sold out. "I chose silver over a dead man's words." He is the betrayer, and his death is shame, not defiance.
- **Bardanes** (Act III): commander of the company in the Sawad. "Turn back, guard. Those Pages are going to Constantinople." He fouls the canal to starve the village of water.

## Act structure
1. **The Caravan.** Riders in Byzantine mail on the dunes; the attack at dusk stays off screen. Jabir falls. Salim wakes in the village with Ishaq, and the chest is gone.
2. **The Kilns.** Olbianos burns pages in the kilns to buy his life. Salim saves what he can.
3. **The Arch.** Bardanes cuts the water. The duel. The Pages are recovered from his tent.
4. **Epilogue: the lamps.** At dusk the village sets lamps on the canal for Jabir and the guards. Ishaq reads one line of the Teacher, and the card closes the chronicle.

## Tone rules
- Grief is shown with restraint: a held look, a lamp, a name spoken aloud.
- No villain speech lasts longer than one sentence.
- Every act ends on a human cost, not a victory fanfare.

## Acts IV to VI and the Hamrin (Round 23 text)
4. **The Marshes.** The chest from Bardanes' tent is light. Kallinikos, master of the company's liquid-fire siphons, fled east into the Nahrawan marshes with the rest. Katakylas (the reed stockade) gives up Petronas (the sunken village), and Petronas gives up Kallinikos (the old weir). Kallinikos: "Too late. I sent the last bundle up the canal at dawn. To Krateros, in al-Karkh. He will burn it before he lets your caliph's men take it back." His own boats carry Salim to Baghdad.
5. **Al-Karkh.** The burned market suburb. Arsaber's men here answer to Krateros. Narses (the burned quarter) gives up Kalokyros (the vaults under the paper-sellers' lane), who says Krateros took the Pages to the square: if he cannot keep them, he will burn them. Krateros: "If Constantinople cannot have them, no one will." After the fight the Pages are taken unburned off the pyre. In the khan, Ishaq gives them to Hakam, a scholar of the House of Wisdom: "We will copy them, ten times over, for ten cities." Hakam's copyists begin that night.
6. **The River Quays.** Arsaber himself holds the quays, with a ship waiting to carry the Pages north. Rhentakios (the shipyard) gives up Skleros (the hulks, holding the copyists' barge), and Skleros says Arsaber cut the bridge of boats and his ship waits at its foot. Arsaber: "Your caliph's sons are busy killing each other, guard. Who will stop me?" After the fight his men throw down their bows and the barge sails downriver: "Two copies to Wasit, two to Basra." Ishaq: "Let Constantinople try to gather them now." At dusk Salim sets lamps on the Tigris: "Jabir. It is done." Ishaq: "We keep the account."
7. **The Hamrin hills (endgame).** What is left of the company holds the last fortified road toward the frontier: the quarry, the cliff fort, the gorge bridge, and Tatzates' hold. Tatzates: "It was only ever the pay."

## Who stays Abbasid
Salim, Ishaq, Hakam, the hub people (Yusuf, Bishr, 'Amr, Kathir, the side-quest givers), the hired guards (Ma'n, Dirar, Tamim, Talha), the four classes, the music, the maps and the holds' layouts.

## Round 25: choices, voices, letters
- **Three choices** (in the scenes; the first option is taken if a scene is skipped before it):
  1. **Photeinos** is beaten, not killed. Bind him for the qadi in Baghdad (+15 Renown; the ending: his testimony names the envoy before the court) or let him go (on the quays a purse waits, signed "P.": "One page was enough"; the ending: he copies letters for a scribe in Wasit).
  2. **Behind the weir the reed village burns** after Kallinikos falls. Chase the bundle that night (+10 Renown; not one Page burns) or stay and fight the fire (a legendary gift from the marsh in al-Karkh; a few Pages' edges catch and are mended from memory; the ending: they named a boat for Salim).
  3. **The parley:** refuse Arsaber, or promise him a copy, freely given. If promised, Salim keeps his word at the end ("Then I go home with a book, and not a theft") and Arsaber goes home with a copy.
- **Ishaq's confession** (the first talk outside the Sawad): he hid the chest in the caravan because no one searches a guard's mules; he chose Jabir's road. Salim answers either way; at the lamps Ishaq says "I chose his road. I will not forget it." and Salim: "Neither will I. Light the next one, Ishaq."
- **Salim's grief,** a line as each region opens: Two more days to Baghdad / Jabir hated boats / he wanted to see the paper-sellers' lane / he would have counted every barge / Tatzates is up here somewhere.
- **The hired guards speak** after fights, at ambushes, champions, bosses and low health. The hub people (Yusuf, Bishr, 'Amr) have a line for each act.
- **Leaves and Letters:** twelve pieces of writing, three per story region: burned leaves of the Pages (secular sayings), soldiers' letters home, and Arsaber's dispatches to the Logothete, which show him doubting the burning. All twelve: the Teacher's Inkwell.
- **Champions:** Rhaptes (Sawad), Kontos (marsh), Mylonas (al-Karkh), Karykes (the quays), each camped beside the main road with his men.

## Round 26: the Hamrin hills and the camp
- **The frontier road (Hamrin):** the hill men bring in one of Tatzates' scouts, bound; he names the four holds and the ravine. Ishaq warns Salim that the end of the road will not give Jabir back; Salim answers that he goes "so that it ends". After two holds fall, Tatzates sends an arrow into the camp with a message ("Come to the ravine, guard. I will not hide from you."). In the ravine: "The boy on the dune. I remember the wind that day. Not his face." / "I remember it for both of us."
- **Tatzates' fall:** he is beaten, not killed, and kneels. Salim chooses: **chains** (he is taken down the Diyala to answer before the qadi in Baghdad) or **a cut bowstring** (he walks north toward the frontier and is not seen again). Either way, that night Salim sets a lamp on the Diyala for Jabir. Ishaq: "A qadi, not a blade" / "We keep the account by remembering, not by killing."
- **Letters in the hills:** a soldier's letter about the frontier and the silver they no longer have; a leaf of the Pages ("Count the stars you can name. Then count the ones you cannot."); Tatzates' quiver lid, with one mark labelled "the boy on the dune".
- **The camp's own stories:** Yusuf's partner Sulayk, lost in the siege, turns up alive in Wasit; Bishr's debtor, a soldier of the Abna', comes back wounded and pays the other half of a sword's price; 'Amr, who fought for al-Amin on the losing side, takes on a camp boy, Nasim, to hold a line "for the camp's wells".
- **New troops:** the bandophoros (a plain unit banner, no device), the shield wall, the hippotoxotes (horse archer).

## Round 32: story everywhere
- **Arsaber's chapter:** in the marshes Salim overhears him and Kallinikos across the water ("We came for books, not for a burned country"); in al-Karkh he meets Salim alone and unarmed in the ash of the paper-sellers' lane, for an hour's truce, and warns him that Krateros will not wait; in the Hamrin camp Rafi' the courier brings his letter from Constantinople (it follows the parley choice) with a word about Tatzates. Two more of his dispatches lie in every region (Things Found), down to his last order disbanding the company and Tatzates' answer scratched on its back.
- **The acts:** a memory at the caravanserai gate (Jabir at seventeen, Salim at twelve: "Loads come back. Hands do not."); Muhalhil, an elder of the reed village, counts what the war has taken; Ishaq tells of the Teacher's last lesson, about lamps, and he and Salim agree to write their dead men's words down; Sumayr the ferryman pays Jabir's eight dirhams to the copyists' lamp oil. After Krateros: the brothers' war ends and Baghdad counts its losses. At the very end: six years on, the new caliph comes home and the House of Wisdom grows.
- **People who come back:** Niketas, an old deserter of the Rum (the Sawad, the marshes, the quays, then the Hamrin flock quest); Lubna and her son Shabib, siege refugees walking home to al-Karkh (their cart on the road, a boat in the marshes, their burned house, then the quays, where Shabib learns his letters and offers to teach Salim); Rafi' the courier, who brings news of Salim's choices a region later.
- **The hired guards' stories,** told while walking: Ma'n (Tahir's man, who threw fire into al-Karkh), Dirar (a reed boy who has never seen the sea), Tamim (a naffat who wants to sell lamp oil), Nahshal (an 'ayyar of the Harbiyya; he was Talha before Round 32). Each ends in the epilogue.
- **The Rum speak:** short orders in fights, the hold masters at their doors, and sixteen letters found on fallen captains (Letters of the Rum).
- **New choices:** Niketas (the qadi, or let him hide), where the first copies go (the House of Wisdom, or the paper-sellers' market too; Ishaq and Hakam disagree), and Arsaber's crews (the exchange on the Lamis, or mending the bridge of boats). Each changes later scenes, the ending's captions and the camp men's farewells.
- **Things Found:** twenty pieces of writing and objects, four per region, kept in the Codex with the Leaves and Letters and the Letters of the Rum. All twenty: The Account Kept.

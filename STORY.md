# Madinat al-Salam: story bible (Round 11)

The inspiration is Shia storytelling, carried by **themes only**. There are no names of holy figures, no rituals, no religious buildings, symbols or text.

## Themes
- **Standing with the wronged against the powerful.** The strong want silence; the weak have only memory.
- **Loyalty that costs everything.** Some keep faith with a dead man's cause; others sell it for bread.
- **Remembrance as resistance.** To speak the names of the fallen is to refuse the tyrant's version of events.
- **Thirst.** Denying water is the cruellest act in the story.
- **Awaited justice.** The scales will be set right one day. Until then we keep the account.

## The secret of the caravan
Ishaq's instruments were not the only cargo. A small cedar chest held the **Pages**, the collected sayings and letters of a gentle scholar called only **the Teacher**. He died in a prison by the Tigris fourteen years ago, in 799, and his students say he was poisoned.

A powerful man in Baghdad, never named, wants the Pages burned. He paid Ghassan to make sure they never reach the city. Ishaq was the Teacher's student.

## Characters
- **Salim:** a caravan guard. He is practical, quiet, and not a believer in causes until the cause is his brother's last request.
- **Jabir:** Salim's older brother and captain of the caravan guard. He dies in the ambush with an arrow in the chest. His last words are "The chest. Do not let them burn it."
- **Ishaq:** an astronomer. He grieves without spectacle, and his line is "We keep the account."
- **Farud** (Act I): a hired raider. He took the gold, then read a page by firelight and never slept well again. His dying confession names the buyer: Ghassan.
- **Hisham** (Act II): the Teacher's former student, who sold out. "I chose bread over a dead man's words." He is the betrayer, and his death is shame, not defiance.
- **Ghassan** (Act III): the tyrant's instrument. "Words make men stubborn. Stubborn men do not pay." He fouls the canal to starve the village of water.

## Act structure
1. **The Caravan.** Ambush. Jabir falls. Salim wakes in the village with Ishaq, and the chest is gone.
2. **The Kilns.** Hisham burns pages in the kilns to buy his life. Salim saves what he can.
3. **The Arch.** Ghassan cuts the water. The duel. The Pages are recovered from his tent.
4. **Epilogue: the lamps.** At dusk the village sets lamps on the canal for Jabir and the guards. Ishaq reads one line of the Teacher, and the card closes the chronicle.

## Tone rules
- Grief is shown with restraint: a held look, a lamp, a name spoken aloud.
- No villain speech lasts longer than one sentence.
- Every act ends on a human cost, not a victory fanfare.

## Round 15: Acts IV and V
4. **The Marshes.** The chest from Ghassan's tent is light. Rawh, his paymaster, fled east into the Nahrawan marshes with the rest. Marwan (the reed camp) gives up Sahl (the fish racks), and Sahl gives up Rawh (the old weir). Rawh: "Too late. I sent the last bundle up the canal at dawn." His own boats carry Salim to Baghdad.
5. **Al-Karkh.** The burned market suburb. The buyer's men answer to 'Utba. 'Asim (the burned suq) gives up Layth (the paper-sellers' lane), who says 'Utba will burn the Pages in the square at sunset. After the fight the Pages are taken unburned off the pyre. In the khan, Ishaq gives them to Hakam, a scholar of the House of Wisdom: "We will copy them, ten times over, for ten cities." At dusk Salim sets lamps on the canal: "Jabir. It is done." Ishaq: "We keep the account."
The buyer himself stays unnamed.

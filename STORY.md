# Madinat al-Salam: story bible (Round 11; Round 23: the Byzantine mission)

The inspiration is Shia storytelling, carried by **themes only**. There are no names of holy figures, no rituals, no religious buildings, symbols or text.

## Themes
- **Standing with the wronged against the powerful.** The strong want silence; the weak have only memory.
- **Loyalty that costs everything.** Some keep faith with a dead man's cause; others sell it for bread.
- **Remembrance as resistance.** To speak the names of the fallen is to refuse the tyrant's version of events.
- **Thirst.** Denying water is the cruellest act in the story.
- **Awaited justice.** The scales will be set right one day. Until then we keep the account.

## The secret of the caravan
Ishaq's instruments were not the only cargo. A small cedar chest held the **Pages**, the collected sayings and letters of a gentle scholar called only **the Teacher**. He died in a prison by the Tigris fourteen years ago, in 799, and his students say he was poisoned. Ishaq was the Teacher's student.

## The frame (Round 23)
While the caliph's two sons fight over Baghdad, an envoy from Constantinople, **Arsaber**, slips into Iraq under the smoke of their war with a picked company of soldiers and hired turncoats. His orders are to carry the best learning of Baghdad home to Constantinople, and the Teacher's Pages are the prize. (Historically no Byzantine army reached Baghdad in 813, which is why this is a covert mission and not an invasion.) The civil war stays the backdrop, not the enemy. Every adversary is Byzantine (al-Rum): period Greek names, no saints' or biblical names, single names without epithets.

## Characters
- **Salim:** a caravan guard. He is practical, quiet, and not a believer in causes until the cause is his brother's last request.
- **Jabir:** Salim's older brother and captain of the caravan guard. He dies in the ambush with an arrow in the chest, loosed by Tatzates. His last words are "The chest. Do not let them burn it."
- **Ishaq:** an astronomer. He grieves without spectacle, and his line is "We keep the account."
- **Arsaber:** the envoy. He came for learning the way other men come for gold. He holds the river quays himself in Act VI.
- **Tatzates:** an Armenian bowman in Byzantine pay, who shot Jabir. "Bardanes paid. I shot. That is all it was." He escapes three times and makes his last stand in his Hamrin hold.
- **Photeinos** (Act I): one of the company. He took the envoy's gold, then read a page by firelight and never slept well again. His dying words name Bardanes and Olbianos; the banner names the envoy, Arsaber.
- **Olbianos** (Act II): a Greek copyist who once sat at the Teacher's lessons, and sold out. "I chose silver over a dead man's words." He is the betrayer, and his death is shame, not defiance.
- **Bardanes** (Act III): commander of the company in the Sawad. "Turn back, guard. Those Pages are going to Constantinople." He fouls the canal to starve the village of water.

## Act structure
1. **The Caravan.** Riders in Byzantine mail on the dunes; the attack at dusk stays off screen. Jabir falls. Salim wakes in the village with Ishaq, and the chest is gone.
2. **The Kilns.** Olbianos burns pages in the kilns to buy his life. Salim saves what he can.
3. **The Arch.** Bardanes cuts the water. The duel. The Pages are recovered from his tent.
4. **Epilogue: the lamps.** At dusk the village sets lamps on the canal for Jabir and the guards. Ishaq reads one line of the Teacher, and the card closes the chronicle.

## Tone rules
- Grief is shown with restraint: a held look, a lamp, a name spoken aloud.
- No villain speech lasts longer than one sentence.
- Every act ends on a human cost, not a victory fanfare.

## Acts IV to VI and the Hamrin (Round 23 text)
4. **The Marshes.** The chest from Bardanes' tent is light. Kallinikos, master of the company's liquid-fire siphons, fled east into the Nahrawan marshes with the rest. Katakylas (the reed stockade) gives up Petronas (the sunken village), and Petronas gives up Kallinikos (the old weir). Kallinikos: "Too late. I sent the last bundle up the canal at dawn. To Krateros, in al-Karkh. He will burn it before he lets your caliph's men take it back." His own boats carry Salim to Baghdad.
5. **Al-Karkh.** The burned market suburb. Arsaber's men here answer to Krateros. Narses (the burned quarter) gives up Kalokyros (the vaults under the paper-sellers' lane), who says Krateros took the Pages to the square: if he cannot keep them, he will burn them. Krateros: "If Constantinople cannot have them, no one will." After the fight the Pages are taken unburned off the pyre. In the khan, Ishaq gives them to Hakam, a scholar of the House of Wisdom: "We will copy them, ten times over, for ten cities." Hakam's copyists begin that night.
6. **The River Quays.** Arsaber himself holds the quays, with a ship waiting to carry the Pages north. Rhentakios (the shipyard) gives up Skleros (the hulks, holding the copyists' barge), and Skleros says Arsaber cut the bridge of boats and his ship waits at its foot. Arsaber: "Your caliph's sons are busy killing each other, guard. Who will stop me?" After the fight his men throw down their bows and the barge sails downriver: "Two copies to Wasit, two to Basra." Ishaq: "Let Constantinople try to gather them now." At dusk Salim sets lamps on the Tigris: "Jabir. It is done." Ishaq: "We keep the account."
7. **The Hamrin hills (endgame).** What is left of the company holds the last fortified road toward the frontier: the quarry, the cliff fort, the gorge bridge, and Tatzates' hold. Tatzates: "It was only ever the pay."

## Who stays Abbasid
Salim, Ishaq, Hakam, the hub people (Yusuf, Bishr, 'Amr, Kathir, the side-quest givers), the hired guards (Ma'n, Dirar, Tamim, Talha), the four classes, the music, the maps and the holds' layouts.
